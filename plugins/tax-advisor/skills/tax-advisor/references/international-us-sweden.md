# U.S. International Tax and Sweden Reference

Use for U.S. citizens, green-card holders, or U.S. residents with foreign residence, income, tax, accounts, assets, pensions, entities, or trusts. The Sweden sections supplement—not replace—the general U.S. workflow.

> Verify every filing-year amount and deadline against the tax-year version of the cited form instructions. Treaty interpretation, PFICs, foreign pensions/entities/trusts, and late international forms usually warrant a U.S.–Sweden specialist.

## First principles

1. **Worldwide income still goes on the U.S. return.** U.S. citizens and resident aliens abroad generally use the same filing rules as taxpayers in the United States. The foreign tax credit (FTC) offsets qualifying double tax; it does not make foreign income disappear. [IRS: U.S. citizens and residents abroad](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-citizens-and-residents-abroad-filing-requirements)
2. **Source income before crediting tax.** Compensation is generally sourced where the services are physically performed, regardless of employer or payment-account location. Swedish workdays usually produce foreign-source compensation; U.S. workdays may produce U.S.-source compensation. [IRS Pub. 514](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p514)
3. **FTC is limited, categorized, and tax-specific.** A foreign levy must be imposed on the taxpayer, paid or accrued, be the legal and actual liability, and qualify as an income tax or tax in lieu of one. Refundable or non-income amounts are not automatically creditable. [IRS Pub. 514](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p514); [Form 1116 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116)
4. **Income-tax relief and information reporting are separate.** An FTC does not satisfy FBAR, Form 8938, PFIC, foreign-entity, trust, pension, or other reporting duties.
5. **Treaties usually do not switch off citizenship taxation.** The U.S.–Sweden treaty saving clause generally preserves U.S. taxation of U.S. citizens, with specific exceptions and double-tax relief under Article 23.

## Intake: facts that control the answer

Ask only what is relevant, but do not calculate an international result without resolving material items:

- tax year, U.S. citizenship/green-card/residency status, and any expatriation issue;
- country of residence, treaty residence, domicile/state ties, move dates, and days worked in each country;
- employer identity and location, work locations, equity-compensation service periods, self-employment, and permanent-establishment/business facts;
- each income item by character and source: wages, bonus, RSUs/options, interest, dividends, capital gains, rental, pension, social security, business, and U.S.-source income;
- Swedish tax by type: preliminary withholding, additional payments, final liability, refunds/credits, tax year, date, and SEK amount;
- prior Form 1116s and Schedule B carryovers by separate category;
- Form 2555/FEIE elections and revocations;
- foreign accounts and maximum aggregate values; ISK, kapitalförsäkring (KF), Swedish funds, companies, trusts, and pensions;
- spouse's U.S. tax status and any election to treat a nonresident spouse as a U.S. resident.

## Foreign tax credit workflow

### 1. Establish the U.S. tax base

- Report worldwide income in U.S. dollars under U.S. characterization and timing rules.
- Reconcile Swedish gross pay to U.S. wages; do not assume Swedish taxable income equals U.S. taxable income.
- Allocate mixed-location compensation by workdays or the rule applicable to the item. Equity compensation may require grant-to-vest or grant-to-exercise service-day sourcing.
- Identify deductions that must be allocated to foreign-source income for Form 1116. Do not use gross foreign income in the limitation numerator when the form requires taxable income.

### 2. Test each Swedish levy

For each amount, document:

- the tax is imposed on this taxpayer;
- it was paid or accrued under the elected method;
- it is the legal and actual liability after available refunds, abatements, and treaty remedies;
- it is an income tax or qualifying tax in lieu of income tax;
- it relates to income included in U.S. taxable income;
- it is not a social contribution, penalty, interest, property tax, wealth/capital tax, or other noncreditable levy merely labeled “tax.”

The treaty's technical explanation identifies Swedish state income tax, special income taxes, and communal/municipal income tax as covered income taxes, while distinguishing capital tax and the insurance-premium excise. Modern levies still require current-law confirmation. [U.S.–Sweden treaty documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/sweden-tax-treaty-documents), Articles 2 and 23.

### 3. Choose and consistently apply paid or accrued

- **Paid method:** generally translate tax at the exchange rate when paid or withheld.
- **Accrued method:** a cash-method individual may elect to claim foreign taxes when accrued; the election generally applies in later years. Accrued taxes generally use the average exchange rate for the tax year, subject to Form 1116 exceptions.
- Swedish preliminary withholding and the later `slutskattebesked` may differ. Reconcile withholding, final liability, refunds, and additional payments rather than crediting every payroll deduction.
- A later refund or final-assessment change can cause a foreign tax redetermination. Determine whether an amended return and revised Form 1116 are required; current Form 1116 instructions also address Schedule C reporting.

Do not casually switch methods to maximize a single year. Compare timing, consistency, SEK/USD effects, and amendment burden.

### 4. Separate Form 1116 categories

Common categories include:

- **General category:** wages and most active/self-employment income not in another category.
- **Passive category:** most interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and certain gains, subject to look-through and high-tax rules.
- **Certain income re-sourced by treaty:** use when the treaty's double-tax relief requires U.S.-source income to be treated as foreign source.
- Additional categories apply to foreign branches, section 951A income, section 901(j) income, and lump-sum distributions.

Prepare a separate Form 1116 computation for each applicable category. Do not pool wage taxes with passive investment taxes merely because Sweden assessed one final tax bill; allocate tax to the income on which it was imposed.

The small-credit election without Form 1116 is limited to passive income reported on qualified payee statements and a tax-year threshold stated in the Form 1116 instructions. Swedish wages generally do not qualify for that shortcut.

### 5. Compute the limitation

Conceptually, for each category:

```text
foreign-source taxable income in category
÷ worldwide taxable income for FTC purposes
× pre-credit U.S. income tax eligible for the FTC
= tentative FTC limit

allowed FTC = lesser of creditable foreign tax or tentative limit
```

Use the actual Form 1116 rules. Qualified dividends, capital gains/losses, itemized or standard deductions, FEIE, foreign/domestic losses, AMT, and treaty re-sourcing can change the fraction or require adjustments.

Excess qualifying tax is generally carried back 1 year and forward 10 years, tracked by category on Schedule B (Form 1116). Verify the tax-year instructions before stating availability. An FTC cannot offset taxes outside its statutory scope, and it does not by itself eliminate U.S. self-employment or social-security obligations.

### 6. Reconcile to the return

Tie the workpaper to:

- Form 1040 worldwide income and deductions;
- every Form 1116 and Schedule B/C;
- Form 2555, if used;
- the Swedish return, final tax decision, payroll withholding, and tax account;
- exchange-rate source and dates;
- state return, if U.S. domicile or source rules keep a state filing obligation alive.

## U.S.–Sweden treaty sequence

### Swedish-source income

For wages earned while working in Sweden, domestic U.S. sourcing usually already treats the income as foreign source. Article 23 supports a U.S. credit for qualifying Swedish income tax, subject to U.S. law and limits. Use general-category Form 1116 unless another category applies.

### U.S.-source income taxed by Sweden

U.S. dividends, interest, capital gains, pensions, or U.S. workdays can require a different sequence:

1. Determine each country's treaty taxing right as if the taxpayer were not relying only on U.S. citizenship.
2. Apply Sweden's credit for U.S. tax to the extent required under Article 23(2) or 23(3)(a).
3. Compute the residual Swedish tax after that credit.
4. Apply the U.S. credit rules under Article 23(3)(b).
5. Re-source only the amount necessary under Article 23(3)(c), using the **certain income re-sourced by treaty** Form 1116 category.

Do not place all U.S.-source income in the treaty category automatically. Preserve each country's primary taxing right and obtain the Swedish-side credit computation. Treaty-based return positions may also require Form 8833; check its exceptions and instructions rather than assuming it is or is not required.

### Saving clause and notable exceptions

- The saving clause generally permits the United States to tax a U.S. citizen resident in Sweden under U.S. domestic law.
- Article 23 relief from double taxation is excepted from the saving clause.
- The 1994 technical explanation states that Swedish social-security-system payments covered by Article 19(2) paid to a U.S. citizen resident in Sweden are taxable only by Sweden.
- Private and occupational pensions are more nuanced: the saving clause can preserve U.S. taxation even when the general pension article assigns residence-country taxation. Analyze the plan, contributions, distributions, and reporting separately.

Use the treaty text, 1994 technical explanation, and 2005 protocol/technical explanation together. [IRS treaty documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/sweden-tax-treaty-documents)

## FTC versus foreign earned income exclusion

Do not default to Form 2555 merely because the taxpayer lives abroad. Compare at least:

| Issue | FTC / Form 1116 | FEIE / Form 2555 |
|---|---|---|
| Core effect | Credits qualifying foreign tax, limited by category | Excludes qualifying foreign earned income up to annual limits |
| Qualification | Creditable tax on foreign-source taxable income | Foreign tax home plus bona fide residence or 330-full-day physical-presence test |
| Swedish high-tax wages | Often favorable, but calculate | May waste tax credits on excluded income |
| Tax on excluded income | N/A | No FTC or deduction for tax allocable to excluded income |
| Investment income | Potential passive-category FTC | Not foreign earned income |
| Election effects | Paid/accrued consistency and carryovers | Revocation can restrict a new election for 5 years without IRS approval |
| Other interactions | Category limits and carryovers | Stacking rule; may affect refundable credits and other benefits |

A hybrid can be valid when only part of wages is excluded, but foreign taxes must be allocated and the excluded portion cannot generate an FTC. In high-tax Sweden, model FTC-only, FEIE-only, and any supportable hybrid using the same income, deductions, exchange rates, and family credits.

## Foreign accounts and assets

### FBAR (FinCEN Form 114)

A U.S. person generally files when the **aggregate** value of foreign financial accounts exceeds **$10,000 at any time** during the calendar year. It is filed separately with FinCEN, due April 15 with an automatic extension to October 15 under current rules. Confirm account scope, ownership, joint accounts, and signature authority. [FinCEN FBAR](https://www.fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts)

### Form 8938

Form 8938 is attached to the income-tax return and does not replace FBAR. Current thresholds are higher for qualifying taxpayers living abroad; verify status, residence, filing status, year-end value, and peak value using the tax-year instructions. [IRS comparison of Form 8938 and FBAR](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/comparison-of-form-8938-and-fbar-requirements)

### Swedish products and U.S. characterization

- **ISK (investeringssparkonto):** Swedish standard-income taxation does not create U.S. tax deferral or replace U.S. gain, dividend, and interest reporting. Identify every underlying holding. Swedish `schablonintäkt` tax creditability and matching to U.S. income can be difficult; do not promise an FTC without analysis.
- **Swedish mutual funds/ETFs:** many non-U.S. pooled funds are PFICs for U.S. purposes, potentially requiring Form 8621 for each fund and punitive default taxation. Screen before purchase and during document review.
- **KF (kapitalförsäkring):** determine whether U.S. law treats it as insurance, an account owned by the taxpayer, a foreign trust, or another arrangement. Review underlying PFICs and possible excise/information reporting with a specialist.
- **Swedish occupational/private pensions:** do not label them “like a 401(k).” Determine treaty treatment, U.S. tax deferral, basis, employer contributions, FBAR/Form 8938 treatment, and any Forms 3520/3520-A exception before reporting.
- **Foreign companies, partnerships, sole proprietorships, and trusts:** screen Forms 5471, 8865, 8858, 3520/3520-A, 926, and related CFC/GILTI issues. Penalties can apply even when no income tax is due.

## Social taxes and totalization

Income-tax FTC analysis does not resolve social-security coverage. Under the U.S.–Sweden totalization agreement, employees generally pay into the system of the country where they work, with rules for temporary assignments; self-employed workers generally follow residence and transfer rules. Obtain a certificate of coverage when required. [SSA: U.S.–Sweden agreement](https://www.ssa.gov/international/Agreement_Pamphlets/sweden.html)

Swedish employer/social contributions are not automatically the employee's creditable income tax. Classify each levy and who legally bears it.

## Deadlines and compliance check

- A calendar-year U.S. taxpayer whose tax home and abode are outside the United States and Puerto Rico on the regular due date generally receives an automatic 2-month filing extension, but interest on unpaid income tax generally runs from the regular April deadline. Confirm the year's dates and extension statement requirements in Pub. 54.
- Form 4868 can extend filing further under ordinary rules; Form 2350 is for certain taxpayers needing time to qualify for FEIE. Extensions to file generally do not extend time to pay.
- FBAR has its own April/October timeline.
- Swedish return and final-tax dates vary. Use [Skatteverket's English filing guidance](https://www.skatteverket.se/declaringtaxes) and the taxpayer's actual `slutskattebesked`.
- If prior U.S. returns or international forms are missing, stop ordinary optimization and assess delinquent-filing options. Do not recommend quiet disclosure.

## Documents to request for a Sweden case

Request only what the calculation needs, with redaction:

- U.S. prior return, Form 1116s, Schedules B/C, Form 2555, and FTC carryover workpaper;
- Swedish `Inkomstdeklaration`, `slutskattebesked`, tax-account transactions, and any foreign-tax-credit decision;
- annual payroll statement and payslips showing gross pay, preliminary income tax, and social charges;
- workday/travel calendar and equity grant/vest/exercise history;
- year-end and maximum-balance statements for each foreign account;
- ISK/KF holdings and transaction-level activity, not merely the account's Swedish taxable standard income;
- pension plan terms and annual statements;
- SEK/USD conversion workpaper with source and date methodology.

## Output checklist

A Sweden/FTC answer should state:

- worldwide U.S. income included and how each item was sourced;
- qualifying and nonqualifying Swedish tax by type;
- paid/accrued method and exchange rates;
- Form 1116 category, limitation, allowed credit, and carryover;
- FTC-versus-FEIE comparison when earned income is material;
- treaty-resourcing result for U.S.-source income, if any;
- FBAR, Form 8938, PFIC, pension/entity/trust, and totalization flags;
- U.S., FinCEN, and Swedish deadlines;
- exactly which issue needs cross-border professional review.
